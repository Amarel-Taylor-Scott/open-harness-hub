# Esoteric Industry Source Surfaces

Many valuable primitives will come from industries where public LLM training
coverage is uneven and the useful knowledge is procedural, local, or embedded
in forms, checklists, inspection notes, quoting workflows, and role-specific
terminology.

These verticals should be represented as source-surface seeds before becoming
deployable pipelines:

- automotive manufacturers, dealers, brokers, new-car sales, used-car sales,
  service departments, warranty-adjacent workflows, title/registration, vehicle
  condition review, and recall/defect evidence;
- employment agencies and labor recruiters, including fee rules, placement
  workflows, worker document checklists, contract review, and verified
  government rule changes;
- plumbers, plumbing systems, HVAC contractors, mechanical systems,
  commissioning checklists, permit inspections, and jurisdiction-specific
  code references;
- woodworking, cabinetry, millwork, finish schedules, shop drawings, cut lists,
  safety procedures, and quality-control checklists;
- offshore oil and gas, platform maintenance, safety cases, inspection
  evidence, environmental monitoring, permit obligations, and emergency
  response procedures;
- environmental reviews, public notices, impact categories, mitigation
  commitments, sampling plans, agency comments, and versioned findings.

The key pattern is to keep detailed domain vocabulary in flexible labels,
canonical entities, dimensions, source-surface metadata, and knowledge objects.
Top-level taxonomy fields remain broad enough that new industries can be added
without schema churn.

## Candidate Primitive Sources

Good source surfaces include public forms, official guidance, inspection
checklists, standards excerpts where licensing allows metadata capture, public
agency notices, open datasets, verified publisher feeds, synthetic examples,
and user-supplied private documents that remain tenant-bound.

Do not ingest proprietary manuals, customer records, private emails, or real PII
into public catalog objects. Private deployments can index those behind tenant
boundaries with separate trust and privacy metadata.
