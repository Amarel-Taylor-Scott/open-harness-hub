# Procedure Knowledge Objects

Every repeatable review procedure can be represented as a knowledge object: a checklist, decision tree, interview script, investigation question set, escalation rule, evidence requirement, or closeout template.

This is valuable for AML analysts, SOC analysts, claims reviewers, clinical triage teams, public health reviewers, procurement reviewers, trust and safety teams, and any workflow where a human reviews alerts generated by another system.

## Alert Review Pattern

Most alert review workflows have the same structure:

1. receive alert payload and model or rules-engine reason codes;
2. retrieve the applicable procedure, checklist, policy, and prior examples;
3. ask required triage questions;
4. gather evidence from systems of record;
5. map observations to known typologies or control failures;
6. decide close, monitor, escalate, file, suppress, or request more information;
7. produce an audit-ready narrative with citations and non-speculative language;
8. evaluate whether the analyst followed the required procedure.

For AML, this means an alert is not only “reviewed by an LLM.” It is walked through a specific institution-approved set of questions: sanctions first, customer profile fit, transaction graph fit, typology match, source-of-funds evidence, unusual geography, velocity, structuring indicators, beneficial ownership, prior alerts, and escalation thresholds.

## Object Types

Useful procedure knowledge objects include:

- `review_question`: a question the analyst or pipeline must answer.
- `checklist_item`: a required control, evidence check, or policy step.
- `decision_gate`: a branch that routes the case based on evidence.
- `evidence_requirement`: required source system, document, field, or citation.
- `typology_mapping`: a link between observed behavior and a known pattern.
- `narrative_template`: a required output structure.
- `closeout_reason`: allowed closure categories and required explanation.
- `escalation_rule`: conditions that require human, compliance, legal, safety, or regulator review.
- `versioned_fact`: a volatile fact captured with publication date, archive timestamp, source URL, hash, and replacement relationship.

These objects can be embedded, searched, rated, versioned, signed, and composed into pipelines.

## AML Alert Review

An AML analyst review pipeline should use procedure objects to ask questions such as:

- Are there sanctions or PEP matches that halt routine review?
- Does the alert match the customer profile, declared activity, occupation, expected volume, and geography?
- Are the transactions structured, rapidly passed through, circular, round-dollar, high-velocity, or inconsistent with account tenure?
- Are counterparties newly added, high risk, shared across unrelated accounts, or linked through a graph path?
- Are there source-of-funds or source-of-wealth gaps?
- Have previous alerts been closed for similar behavior, and did the behavior persist or change?
- Which typology, rule, policy, or advisory supports the finding?
- What evidence would justify closing the alert without escalation?
- What evidence would require enhanced due diligence, SAR draft preparation, or sanctions escalation?

This turns analyst knowledge into reusable and testable RAG context instead of burying it in training decks and local SOP PDFs.

## Archive-Backed Fact Versioning

Public facts and guidance change. CDC pages, regulator notices, advisories, sanctions lists, and eligibility rules need versioned capture, not just live retrieval.

The platform should support:

- capture lookup through public archive indexes such as the Internet Archive Wayback CDX API;
- optional snapshot request through Save Page Now-style services when allowed;
- private WARC capture for enterprise or government deployments;
- source hashing and diffing between versions;
- effective date and archived date separation;
- citations to both the publisher URL and the archived capture;
- replacement and supersession relationships between facts;
- retrieval policies that prefer the fact version valid for the user’s requested date.

This lets the platform answer: “What did CDC guidance say on this date?” or “Which version of the rule was used when this case was reviewed?”

## Platform Implication

The primitive database should treat procedures, questions, checklists, and archived facts as first-class knowledge objects. A million-component catalog will not only contain tools and pipelines; it will contain the small procedural atoms that make a pipeline verifiable.
