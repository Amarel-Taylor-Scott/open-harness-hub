# Baltor Original Commercial Production System — Artistic Brief

**Production status: concept/storyboard only; no finished commercial in this package.** The previous Baltor motion videos remain separate demonstrations.

## Campaign A — The Unseen Problem (60s)
Emotional human stakes. A tired nonprofit research team misses a quiet signal under a mountain of information; a composable system surfaces evidence and actionable context. Do not imply a system detected real crimes or saved lives unless verified. Ending: `Build once. Help everywhere.` Full shot plan lives at `examples/flagship_superbowl_style_storyboard.json`.
- Lock: restrained midnight indigo → warm dawn ochre, one recurring amber signal dot, asymmetric realistic photography/illustration, no tech-noise overlays in the first 15 seconds.
- Camera: 0–18s patient camera language and increasing compression; at 18s silence and frozen motion; 21–48s accelerating compositional rhythm; 48–60s resolve with generous typography space.
- Audio: scene-authored original score with dynamics rather than robotic TTS. Pursue licensed neural narration or actor recording with approval. Mix to the platform's loudness guidance after checking delivery requirements.
- Continuity: office desks, notebook, coffee cup, four recurring characters, time-of-night progression, amber signal motif; persistent shot and prop IDs.

## Campaign B — The Last Tab (30s)
Comedy. A developer opens 47 tabs to find one reusable capability. The browser windows multiply into absurdity. Smash cut to one clean operator selector with verified metadata, test results and a single-click workflow. End on an original punchline; avoid pretending the interface is fully live if mocked.
- Style: kinetic graphic novel, paper-cut tabs, sharp match cuts, 1.5s micro-scenes, comedic silence before brand reveal.
- CTA: `Stop searching. Start building.`

## Campaign C — The Smallest Part (60s)
Stylized 3D: one small mechanical module travels through distinct worlds—a factory, a hospital research lab, a classroom, and a rescue planning center—helping solve different problems as it composes with different modules. These are allegorical illustrations, not proof of validated applications.
- Style: cinematic low-poly handcrafted world, miniature set lighting, macro lens, coherent physical dimensions, 3D articulated components.
- CTA: `A capability in one world. A possibility in another.`

## Campaign D — The Wrong Alert (45s)
An agent alerts an engineer 36 times about unimportant changes. The engineer dismisses everything, then an actual critical change appears. The system improves only when it tests changes against context and preserves evidence. Show false positives and failures honestly.
- Style: restrained documentary/tech thriller; bold on-screen timestamps and evidence cards.
- CTA: `Less noise. More signal.`

## Campaign E — Let the Computer Read It (30s)
Surreal visual metaphor: an endless conveyor belt of newsletters and social posts streams toward a human, who is trying to manually paste them into an AI chat. The belt redirects directly to agent evaluation lanes.
- Style: high-end motion graphics, graphical comedy, expressive characters, impossible camera perspective.
- CTA: `Don't be the middleman between information and your AI.`

## Multi-format rules
- Master scenes and typography separately; never upscale 540p assets and call them genuine 1080p render masters.
- Produce true 1080x1920/1920x1080/1080x1080 recompositions, not center crops of a single master.
- Ensure at least one uninterrupted subject-led shot in the first five seconds; no template-like uniform boxes or giant distracting borders.
- Captions: 1–2 short lines in safe areas, no subject occlusion, timed by words for final narrated masters.
- Effects: camera intent first; no shake, cuts, zooms or glow without a story beat.
- Sound: matching room tone, Foley, music stem, narration dynamics; loudness and clipping QA.
- Human gate: story review, art/continuity review, frame checks, audio listen-through, platform-safe preview, exact asset/copy approval.
- Automated checks are necessary but not sufficient to call a film “fully polished.”
